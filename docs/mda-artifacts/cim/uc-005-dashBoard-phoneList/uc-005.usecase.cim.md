## Purpose of this document

This document captures the **business need** behind UC-005 at Slottet residential care facility. It is written in domain language only and does **not** describe any system, screen, or technology. It describes how the work happens — and would happen the same way with paper, with a system, or any other implementation.

For the system-level interaction view, see the PIM use case specification.

## Use Case

**Name:** Coordinate Work Phone Assignment for a Shift

**Primary Actor:** Caregiver on Shift

**Supporting Actors:** Shift Coordinator

**Scope:** Slottet — Shift Phone Coordination

**Level:** User goal (Cockburn: blue sea level)

---

## Stakeholders and Interests

| Stakeholder | Interest |
|-------------|----------|
| Caregiver on Shift | Wants to know who carries which phone, and to take responsibility for one phone during the shift |
| Other caregivers | Want to know who to ask when something happens during the shift |
| Shift Coordinator | Wants an overview of who covers which area of responsibility |
| Residents | Indirectly benefit when caregivers can reach each other quickly |
| Slottet management | Wants accountability and continuity across shifts |

---

## Preconditions

- The caregiver is on duty for an active shift (Day, Evening, or Night).
- The fixed set of work phones exists at Slottet.

## Postconditions

**Success guarantee:**  
- The caregiver is identified as the responsible carrier of one specific work phone for the duration of the shift.
- All caregivers on the shift know who carries each phone.

**Minimal guarantee:**  
- No false assignment is recorded. Either the caregiver's responsibility is clear, or it is clearly marked as unknown.

---

## Main Success Scenario (essential flow)

1. A new shift begins at Slottet.
2. The caregiver picks up a work phone for the shift.
3. The caregiver makes the assignment known to colleagues on the shift.
4. Other caregivers and the shift coordinator can identify who carries which phone.
5. At the end of the shift, the caregiver releases responsibility for the phone.

## Alternative Flows

**3a. The caregiver hands the phone to a colleague mid-shift:**
- 1. The original caregiver gives the phone to another caregiver on the same shift.
- 2. The new caregiver becomes the responsible carrier from that moment.

**5a. The shift ends without explicit release:**
- 1. The next shift's caregivers take over the phones as part of the shift handover.
- 2. New assignments are made for the new shift.

---

## Business Rules

- Each work phone is carried by **one** caregiver at a time during a shift.
- A caregiver may carry **at most one** work phone per shift.
- Phone assignments are **per shift** — they do not persist across shifts.
- The set of work phones is fixed and does not change between shifts.
- The phone list is for **identification** — it is not a contact list, and there is no calling, dialing, or messaging function associated with this coordination.

---

## Domain Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Caregiver | Staff member on duty providing care to residents |
| Shift | A working period: Day, Evening, or Night |
| Work phone | A physical phone carried by a caregiver during a shift, used for communication on the premises |
| Shift Coordinator | A caregiver with additional coordinating responsibilities for the shift |
| Shift handover | The transfer of responsibility between an outgoing and incoming shift |
